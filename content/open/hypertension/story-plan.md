# Hypertension: story plan

Written by the statements' writer (Claude) on 24 Nov 2026, from the 95 open-source statements in `statements.csv`. Only statements agreed by two checkers that are not the writer can go in the story; the builder refuses the rest.

1. **What kind of thing is this?** A long-term condition that is mostly found, not felt: the statements are about measuring, confirming, finding a cause, finding damage and bringing it down, with an acute end (emergencies).
2. **The one idea.** Outside a hypertensive crisis, one raised reading is not a diagnosis. Once confirmed outside the clinic: look for a cause where the clues point to one, know what it damages, bring it down. A hypertensive emergency needs immediate attention, with only a partial reduction at first. (Changed 24 Nov after GPT's read: the sources do not say everyone is assessed for damage, so the story does not either.)
3. **Shape: presentation.** It follows a person from a raised reading to treatment, in the order the decisions come up. Not mechanism: the open sources say little about how blood pressure is regulated (checklist item 15 is only partly covered). Not emergency: emergencies are one step at the end. Not comparison: the comparisons inside it (white coat against masked, severe against emergency) are tables within steps.
4. **Mixed up with.** White coat hypertension and masked hypertension, which are mixed up with each other and with true hypertension: a table in step 3. Severe hypertension and a hypertensive emergency: a table in step 10. Pseudo-resistance and resistant hypertension: step 9.
5. **Often got wrong.** None of the statements carries a `misconception` from its source, so there is no "often got wrong" point. The two traps the sources do name are stated as facts in their steps: a cuff over clothing overestimates blood pressure (step 2), and about half of apparent resistant hypertension is non-adherence (step 9).
6. **Arbitrary lists.** None gets a memory aid. The secondary causes have a logic (clue, then test), so they are a table instead.
7. **Pictures or tables.** A threshold table (clinic against home), the white coat and masked table, a secondary causes table (clue and first test), and severe against emergency.
8. **Not told by these sources.** Stages of hypertension, which drug to start first by age, ethnicity and diabetes and the steps after it, not combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB, drug choice in diabetes and kidney disease, and blood tests after a drug change. They are the story's `gaps`, linked to where to read them. Rows 42 (patterns of raised blood pressure in young adults) and 55 (the NHS lifestyle list) are left out because their reworded versions have not been read by two checkers; the lifestyle step stands without 55.

**Statements not used:** 7 (reworded on 24 Nov to carry its quote's cardiovascular-risk warning; it comes back once two checkers have read the new wording), 42, 55 (above), 59 (drug treatment below 160/100 at moderate to high risk, which overlaps the BIHS 2025 position in row 58 and would need explaining against it).
