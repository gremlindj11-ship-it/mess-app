1 | pathophysiology | always | core | OX | b4.s1 | Respiratory failure | is | when the respiratory system fails to carry out its gas exchange functions, which include oxygenation and removing carbon dioxide.

3,22 | pathophysiology | always | core | OX | b4.s4 | Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure, also called type 2, | is | a ventilation failure defined by an arterial carbon dioxide pressure (PaCO2) above 45 mm Hg and a pH below 7.35, indicating respiratory acidosis, and is not included in acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure.

3p,7p | pathophysiology | most | core | HC | b4.s1 | Patients with acute respiratory failure | almost always develop | gas exchange derangements that may result in hypercapnia.
28 | mechanism | may | extension | HC | b36.s1-s4 | Both hypercapnia and hypercapnic acidosis | shift | the haemoglobin–oxygen dissociation curve to the right and may increase haematocrit, increasing oxygen release to the tissues in a canine model; hypercapnia also increases cardiac output through sympathoadrenal mechanisms, with increased preload, afterload, contractility and heart rate, and the overall effect is better oxygenation and improved global oxygen supply.
29 | mechanism | always | extension | HC | b37.s1-s2 | Hypercapnia | lowers | alveolar oxygen tension, by the formula PAO2 = FiO2 (PB − 47) − (PaCO2/R), where PAO2 is the alveolar oxygen tension, PB the barometric pressure and R the respiratory quotient; however, compared with changes in FiO2, the effect of changes in alveolar ventilation on alveolar oxygenation is small.
30 | mechanism | may | extension | HC | b34.s1-s3 | Hypercapnia | may either decrease or increase | airway resistance: alveolar hypercapnia decreases it by relaxing the smooth muscle of small bronchi (a direct effect) in healthy subjects, while systemic hypercapnia causes vagally mediated constriction of the larger airways (an indirect effect) in animal models, and these opposing but balanced effects may produce little net change in airway resistance.
30,6p | mechanism | usually | extension | HC | b35.s1 | Hypercapnia | potentiates | hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction, reducing intrapulmonary shunt and improving gas exchange in normal and diseased lungs.
30 | mechanism | sometimes | extension | HC | b39.s2 | Hypercapnic acidosis | impairs | diaphragmatic function in spontaneously breathing patients, through effects on afferent transmission by the vagal nerve.
