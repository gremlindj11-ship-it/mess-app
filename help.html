Help

Getting started

Open Mess. There's no account. Set it up with your year and exams, or go straight in. You can change everything later in Profile, including hiding any page you don't use.

The best place to start is Topics. Pick a condition, read the notes, then test yourself.

Install it

Installing gives Mess its own icon and lets it open with no internet, after you've opened it once online. It's optional, and it stays free.

Computer (Chrome or Edge)

Open Mess, then click the install icon at the end of the address bar, or go to Profile and press Install Mess if it appears.

iPhone or iPad (Safari)

Open Mess, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Android (Chrome)

Open Mess, tap the menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

Using AI

Cards the AI writes are marked "Made by AI · not checked" until you check them against Mess's notes or your own sources. AI can be wrong in ways that read perfectly well. After you flip one, tap It's right, I've checked or It's wrong, remove it (you can undo). In Anki they're tagged Mess::unchecked_AI.

AI is optional, and you choose it in Profile.

The AI you already use

This works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, Grok, Mistral, DeepSeek and Meta AI, on any plan, with no key.

  1. Press a sparkle on a topic page, for example next to Flashcards.
  2. Choose your AI. Mess copies the prompt and opens it; paste the prompt in. Mess never puts your prompt in a web address, because addresses end up in browser history and server logs.
  3. Send it, copy the whole answer, and paste it into Mess.

Cards, questions, notes and diagrams can all be made this way.

Connect with a key

A key lets the AI work right inside Mess, including the AI patient, the consultant and answers in the ? panel. Pick a company in Profile, make a key on its site, paste it in and press Test. Your key stays in your browser and only goes to that company. Some offer free keys, though offers change, and some companies may block use from a browser. If so, use the first option instead.

Anything else works too, if it has an OpenAI-style address. Choose Anything else and add the address and model. For Ollama on your own computer, try http://localhost:11434/v1.

No AI

Notes, your own cards and questions, cases, scores, calculators and links all work without it.

AI can be wrong. Check what it writes before you learn it.

Topics and notes

  • Edit a note: press the pencil. To undo it, press Back to the original.
  • Add a topic: press + on Topics, or search for it and tap Add. It gets the same page as any other.
  • Add a card or question: press + next to Flashcards or Questions on the topic page. The form stays open so you can add several.
  • Links: anything you write using a topic's name or short form gets tagged to it automatically.
  • In practice: law and ethics, certificates, prevention, safe practice, consultation skills and projects have their own section at the end of the areas list. Each one links to the conditions and drugs it comes up with, and they link back.
  • Share your work: the download icon on Topics saves your edits and additions as a file, and the upload icon adds someone else's.

Notes, cards and lectures

Use whatever makes the best notes and cards for you, then bring them into Mess. Press + Add notes or cards on Topics, or drop a file anywhere. Mess files them under your topics, skips anything you already have, and links them to your stats, questions and cases.

  • NotebookLM (free): make Flashcards in Studio, download the CSV and drop it in. It’s the best free option for recordings and YouTube.
  • Quizlet: on a set you made, ⋯, Export, Tab and New line. Paste it in.
  • ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini: press Copy the prompt, paste it into the AI with your notes, and paste its answer back. It lands in the right topics and sections, with no key needed.
  • Anki: File, Export, Notes in Plain Text, then drop it in.

Why was I wrong?

After a wrong answer, press ✦ Why was I wrong? The AI you use says why your answer was tempting, the clue in the question that decides it, and one rule to remember. Keep it as a flashcard so it comes back. It works with any AI, including free ones you paste into.

Questions work the same way. In Practice, Questions, press + Add your own questions. Bring them from ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini with Copy the prompt, a NotebookLM quiz (via the free NotebookLM Tools add-on), or a spreadsheet with Question, A to E, Answer and Explanation columns. They count in your stats like any other question. Never paste questions from Passmed or other banks: add your scores from those in Stats instead.

Or let Mess make them

Drop a lecture onto Mess anywhere, or press + Add notes or cards on Topics. It reads PDF, PowerPoint (.pptx), Word (.docx), text, captions (.vtt, .srt) and recordings. Scans, pictures and handwriting have no text to read, so use NotebookLM for those.

Mess summarises it, sorts it into topics, and gets notes, flashcards and questions ready. You tick what goes in, and anything that looks wrong or out of date is flagged to check. Past lectures stay in the same box. This needs AI.

Going deeper

Notes start simple. Press Go deeper under any section for more detail: mechanisms, numbers, guideline detail and exam traps. Add it to your notes or remove it.

How it works

Mess reads the whole lecture, slide by slide, and maps what it teaches. Then it writes notes and cards a chunk at a time, keeping track of which slides still have no cards. Every card is checked against the slide it came from: yes/no questions, duplicates and cards that only say "see the slide" are left out, and anything it can't match to the slide is flagged for you to check. The bar shows which slides have cards. With the AI you paste into yourself, it does a shorter single pass instead.

Recordings

Add the recording with the slides, or better, its captions file (.vtt or .srt). Panopto, Teams and YouTube can all give you one. Mess matches what the lecturer said to each slide, so things said out loud make it in, and slides they stressed (“this comes up in the exam”) go first. If the lecture is long, slides they skipped are left out of the cards, which keeps it quick. Captions work with any AI. For audio or video, add a free Groq key in Profile: it turns speech into text. Files over 25 MB are shrunk in your browser first, which works best on a computer.

Reading and colours

Press the Aa icon on any notes to choose Plain, Key words bold, Word starts bold or Short lines, plus text size. With AI connected, there's also a Punchy rewrite.

Select any text for Explain, + Card and the colour tools: highlight, underline, text colour and bold, in any colour. The palette icon keeps the tools out so you can keep painting, and the eraser removes colour from whatever you select or tap.

The diagram icon has AI make a flowchart, comparison table or tree of causes and types. You can ask for changes, then save it to the notes or discard it. Skin pages link to real photos on DermNet.

Flashcards and Anki

Cards live in Practice, under Flashcards. Mess schedules reviews with FSRS, the same method as Anki's newer option. After any question, one tap makes it a flashcard.

Cards from a lecture that are already in your Anki

When you bring in cards from a lecture, press See which of these are already in my Anki. Mess looks in your own Anki for cards that say the same thing, shows you each pair, and when you press Add to Mess it tags those Anki cards with the lecture, switches on any that were suspended, and adds only the missing ones to Mess. Undo in Anki puts it back. It needs Anki open on a computer with the AnkiConnect add-on (code 2055492159); it can't work on a phone. The matching is by words, on your computer, so check the pairs. Nothing is sent anywhere.

See your Anki deck on topic pages

In Flashcards, press Read my Anki deck. Mess reads your notes from Anki once, keeps a copy on this computer, and files each note under the topics its words mention. Each topic page then shows how many of your Anki notes mention it, how many are suspended and how many you've forgotten, with buttons to switch the suspended ones on (and undo), or open them in Anki. Press Update to pick up changes. Remove Mess's copy deletes it; your Anki is never changed by that.

More from your Anki deck

Once Mess has read your deck: get a question wrong and it shows your own Anki notes on that answer, with a button to switch a suspended one on. Topic pages list checked guideline facts your deck may be missing, and a fact with a number can become a fill-the-gap card. In Flashcards you can switch on every suspended note in an area, and see which notes use US drug names with the UK name beside them. After a lecture, the new cards can be sent to Anki under the same lecture tag. If you have an AI set up, you can ask it about lecture cards the word check couldn't match; that is the only step that sends card text anywhere, and it says so before you press it. All of this compares words, not meaning, so check what it shows you.

Move your cards to Anki

  1. In Flashcards, press Download for Anki.
  2. In Anki, go to File, Import, and pick the file. They arrive sorted and tagged by topic.

Bring an Anki deck in

  1. In Anki, go to File, Export. Choose Notes in Plain Text, pick the deck and tick Include tags.
  2. In Mess, go to Flashcards, then Choose Anki export.

Cards show on matching topic pages and stay on your device. Pictures don't come across, and cards only join your review if you add them.

Send straight to Anki (computer only)

  1. In Anki, go to Tools, Add-ons, Get Add-ons, paste 2055492159 and restart.
  2. Go to Tools, Add-ons, choose AnkiConnect, then Config, and add Mess's address to the list:
    "webCorsOriginList": ["http://localhost", "https://messmed.co.uk"]
  3. Restart Anki and leave it open, then press Send to Anki in Mess and allow it.

If it won't connect, use the download. It always works.

See your Anki stats

On Stats, press Add Anki stats. In Anki, go to File, then Export, pick Anki Deck Package, tick Include scheduling information, and choose that file in Mess. Mess shows which topics you keep forgetting. Your cards stay in Anki.

Practice

  • OSCE: work through a station with a friend, who reads the script and marks you, or on your own with an AI patient.
  • Questions: pick an area and kind. To stick to one condition, use Do these on its topic page.
  • Prescribing: PSA-style tasks.
  • Consultant: talk things through with AI. This needs a connected key.

Stats

Questions you do here count automatically. To add results from elsewhere, copy your results page from Passmed or any question bank and paste the lot, type a few like Cardio 62, Resp 70, or add a PDF, CSV or text file. You can also log a single question you want to remember, or pull from Anki. Your weakest areas go to the top, and Today uses them too.

Placement

Keep your cases in folders by block, using your own layout. Select words to mark them or turn them into a flashcard. Conditions and drugs you mention link to their topics automatically.

Never write anything that identifies a patient. No names, dates of birth or hospital numbers.

Today and Tools

Today shows your day, your plan and what to work on. Add things by typing, like 3pm teaching 2 hours.

Got something wrong today? Say where (a question, the ward or teaching), type what tripped you up or paste the question, and add the right answer if you have it. That helps Mess most, because questions are mostly the patient's story. Mess opens the step of the topic's story that explains it, with two other likely steps and "none of these" in case it picked wrong, and makes a flashcard for tomorrow from that step. For questions, you can say why you got it wrong: never knew it, couldn't recall it, it looked familiar but you couldn't work it out, you were down to two, you misread it, or the question was wrong. Each one changes what Mess does. Steps remember your mistakes, Stats shows your commonest cause, and Undo removes it all. It stays on your device.

Tools, at the bottom of the side rail, has calculators and reference ranges in boxes you can pull out and keep open.

The ? button

It's in the corner of every page, and it knows what you're looking at. Ask it a question or tell it to do something, and it does it in Mess. Anything it saves can be undone.

  • test me on this
  • make flashcards on asthma
  • add to my calendar 3pm teaching
  • add task: finish cardio questions
  • i got 62 in cardio
  • how am i doing
  • add to my notes: ...

Some of these need AI. Without it, the ? still does what it can and takes you to the right place.

Backups and new devices

Mess saves in the browser you're using, so it doesn't sync between devices, and clearing your browser data clears Mess. Back up often.

  1. In Profile, press Download a backup, and keep the file somewhere safe.
  2. On the new device, open Mess, go to Profile and press Restore a backup.

AI keys aren't in backups, so add them again. Imported Anki decks aren't either, so bring those in again too.

Restored the wrong backup? Press Undo the restore, just under it. It works for 7 days.

Your data

Who runs Mess: Sofia Vidal. Contact: hello@messmed.co.uk.

Mess has no accounts and no server holding your work. Everything you add stays in your browser on your device unless you send it somewhere yourself (see what leaves your device), and we can't see it. Made for medical students. Mess collects no personal data from anyone, apart from any email you choose to send us.

To delete it all: in Mess, go to Profile, open More, and press Delete everything on this device twice. That removes your work, the copies kept for Undo, your AI keys and any decks you brought in. It can't be undone, so download a backup first if you might want it.

What the website counts

This website counts visits, with no cookies and nothing that identifies you. Your work is stored only on this device. It leaves only through the things listed below, like your own AI.

The count covers two pages only: the home page and this Help page. Cloudflare Web Analytics does it, and is told which page opened, the site you came from, your country, your browser and kind of device, and how fast the page loaded. It is never loaded inside Mess, so it never sees anything you read, write or add there.

What leaves your device

  • Your own AI key. If you add one, whatever you type into Mess's AI features goes from your browser straight to the AI company you chose, with the notes, cards or questions that feature works on, and your profile and anything you've asked Mess to remember (your year, university and instructions). Mess never sees it. That company handles it under its own terms and privacy policy, which can include keeping it.
  • Opening your own AI. When you choose Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, Grok or Mistral, Mess opens that site with the prompt in the link, so the site receives the prompt as soon as it opens, before you press send. The prompt can include your year and university if you've set them. With DeepSeek and Meta AI, nothing goes until you paste it.
  • Lecture recordings. If you have Mess transcribe a recording, the audio goes to Groq if you've added a Groq key, otherwise to OpenAI. Patient details can't be taken out of audio, so never record patients.
  • Wikipedia. When a topic has no notes yet, Mess asks Wikipedia for its short summary. Wikipedia sees the topic's name and your IP address, as for any site you visit.
  • Reporting a problem. If you report a problem and choose to email it, your email app opens a message to hello@messmed.co.uk with your report and the name of the page you were on. Nothing goes until you press send.
  • Hosting. Cloudflare hosts messmed.co.uk. Like any web host, it handles your IP address to send you the pages.
  • Inside Claude. If you open Mess inside Claude, its AI and app connections go through Claude, under Anthropic's terms.

Patient details are taken out of text before it goes to an AI (see below), but that catches the usual identifiers, not everything.

Your rights

Mess holds nothing of yours: your work is on your device, and the visit count can't tell who you are. If you email us, we keep your email to reply and to fix what you report. To ask what we hold, have it deleted or correct it, email hello@messmed.co.uk. If you're unhappy with how we handle it, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

What happens to what you bring in

Every file, paste, pack and backup goes through the same checks before Mess uses it.

  • Is it what it says? Mess looks at what a file really is, not just its name, and won't open a web page renamed .pdf or a file that unpacks to something huge.
  • Nothing runs. PDFs open with their scripts off. Diagrams, SVGs, packs and imports are cleaned so nothing in them can run.
  • Patient details come out. NHS numbers (checked properly, so ordinary numbers stay), hospital numbers and dates of birth are taken out of anything you bring in. Before anything goes to an AI, names, addresses, postcodes, phone numbers and emails come out too. Mess tells you when it took something out.
  • Hidden characters go. Invisible characters that can hide instructions or make two identical cards look different are removed.
  • Undo. After bringing something in, press Undo to put things back as they were. It works for 7 days.

It catches the usual identifiers, not everything. Still never write anything that identifies a patient.

If you use AI, what you send goes to the company you chose, under its own terms. With the paste-back option, you see exactly what's sent. If you email a problem report, we'll see what you send.
